The hole concentration profile across a Si bar can be described with $p(x)$, and the electron concentration profile with $n(x)$. From this distribution, the movement of charges produces a diffusion current. 

$D_p$ - Hole diffusion constant ($D_p=12\dfrac{cm^2}{s}$ for intrinsic Si) 
$D_n$ - Electron diffusion constant ($D_n=35\dfrac{cm^2}{s}$ for intrinsic Si)

**Einstein Relation**
The diffusion constants and [[01 School/01.01 Spring 2024/EEC111/Notes/Drift Current#^chargemobility|charge mobility]] are related by the thermal voltage. 
$\dfrac{D_n}{\mu_n}=\dfrac{D_p}{\mu_p}=V_T$
$V_T=\dfrac{kT}{q}$ (~25.9mV at room temperature of $T=300K$)
$k=8.617\times10^{-5}eVK^{-1}$

Diffusion current is proportional to the concentration gradient at a point. 
**Hole diffusion current density** 
$J_{D,p}=-qD_p\dfrac{dp(x)}{dx}$

**Electron diffusion current density**
$J_{D,n}=qD_n\dfrac{dn(x)}{dx}$
